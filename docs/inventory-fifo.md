# FIFO inventory costing and warehouse recovery

The warehouse API persists real quantities, acquisition costs, retail prices, tracking identities, and immutable movements. Sales invoices and purchasing/supplier documents remain outside this implementation.

## Posting and precision

`InventoryService` validates requests and fingerprints their original decimal strings. `InventoryCosting` owns scale-4 money, scale-6 quantity, selected-unit price conversion, overflow checks, and cumulative FIFO rounding. `SqliteInventoryStore` resolves catalog/tracking rows and coordinates the SQLite transaction. Controllers and WinForms contain no costing policy.

A manual receipt requires `unitCost` and `salePrice`, both nonnegative decimal strings with at most four fractional places. `quantity` must be positive and exactly convertible to scale-6 base quantity. Zero price is an explicit valid price; missing price is unknown and is rejected. Prices refer to the **selected product unit**. For example, two cartons of twelve units at purchase price 120 and selling price 180 per carton produce 24 base units, acquisition value 240, and effective sale price 15 per base unit. Retail conversion rounds once, away from zero, to scale 4. Receipt total value is calculated from the entered quantity and entered purchase price, preserving value even when a rational conversion gives a rounded base-unit cost.

`InventoryDocumentLines.EnteredUnitCostScaled` and `EnteredSalePriceScaled` retain the supplied unit prices. The original quantity, conversion ratio, product-unit ID, base quantity, and informational base-unit cost remain on the line. Purchase reference prices are never substituted or overwritten. The selected variant's existing `SellPriceOverrideScaled` is updated atomically; other variants' pricing remains intact. For a product with exactly one variant, the product's base selling price and existing legacy price mirror also advance so the ordinary catalog list shows the new price. For multi-variant products, the product base price stays unchanged. Variant and product aggregate versions advance, and `CatalogAuditLog` links the pricing decision to the receipt document. Product-level reference pricing is still a separate catalog field; a variant override is the effective retail price returned by catalog detail/barcode lookup and inventory valuation.

One transaction writes document/lines, retail-price update/audit, incoming layers or outgoing allocations, movements, balances, physical-unit locations, and inventory audit. Rejected multi-line requests leave none of those effects. Writers serialize through SQLite, retain the bounded busy timeout, and use checked arithmetic. Negative stock is prohibited.

## Layers and allocations

Migration 016 adds `InventoryCostLayers`, immutable `InventoryCostAllocations`, and immutable `InventoryCostReconciliations`, plus the two nullable line-price snapshot columns. No earlier migration is edited. A layer retains warehouse, variant, batch, source movement, parent layer, original acquisition movement/time, initial quantity/value/cost, and remaining quantity/value. Only decreasing the remaining quantity/value is permitted; original metadata cannot be changed or deleted.

An issue selects available layers within the warehouse/variant/batch identity, ordered by original acquisition movement ID, then layer ID. The serialized movement sequence is the authoritative receipt order, so clock corrections cannot reorder acquisitions; acquisition UTC remains recorded for audit. A documented legacy opening basis precedes post-cutover receipts. An explicit lot is still required for a batch-tracked outbound operation; FIFO applies inside that selected stock identity. This is accounting FIFO, not automatic FEFO or serial-specific purchase-cost selection. Physical serial/IMEI validation and location history remain independent and mandatory.

Each withdrawal records its quantity and value against both source layer and outbound movement. Allocation uses the cumulative proportion of the layer's original value, rounded away from zero; final withdrawal removes the exact remaining value. Small successive withdrawals therefore conserve the original total. Overflow aborts the posting rather than converting to floating point or choosing another cost method.

A transfer first allocates the source layers and writes both warehouse movements in the same transaction. It creates one destination layer for each transferred fragment, retaining the parent link, original acquisition ordering and cost basis. It never merges all fragments into an average-cost layer or resets their acquisition time. Receipt 10 at 100 plus receipt 10 at 120 followed by issue 12 leaves exactly 8 at 120, with value 960. Transfer and replay tests assert the same allocations, values and origins.

Positive adjustments and positive count differences require an actual entered acquisition cost and create layers. Shortages, negative adjustments, issues and eligible reversals consume FIFO. A zero count difference creates no layer, allocation, or movement. Existing count version/quantity checks, tracking requirements and restricted reversal rules remain enforced. Integrity checks compare layers with the movement/balance projection; projection rebuild refuses a FIFO mismatch and does not recreate or guess layers.

## Historical compatibility and reconciliation

The old implementation issued at weighted average and did not retain FIFO allocations. Even when an old total value is known, that total does not prove which receipt quantities remained. Migration 016 therefore preserves all balances, values, movements, documents, variants, batches and physical identifiers, and records one `unresolved` quantity/value layer per nonzero existing balance. It does **not** invent historical receipts or label old weighted-average totals as exact FIFO.

An unresolved layer blocks cost-sensitive outbound posting, including transfer and shortages. New fully priced incoming stock may still be received, but the combined balance remains explicitly incomplete for valuation. `costBasisComplete` is false while any unresolved stock or a layer/projection mismatch exists. Retail value and estimated gross profit are also incomplete if required sale prices are missing; unknown amounts are never reported as zero. The UI does not sum unsafe JSON integers into apparently precise totals.

An administrator may use **توثيق التكلفة** on the affected balance. `POST /api/inventory/cost-basis/reconcile` requires session, CSRF, administrator role, operation key, unresolved layer ID, expected balance version, explicit base-unit `unitCost`, and documentary `evidence`. It accepts a documented **opening basis**, not reconstructed historical FIFO. The opening basis takes effect at the migration cutover, before later incoming layers; no claimed pre-cutover receipt order is manufactured. The immutable reconciliation retains actor, evidence, original unresolved layer, replacement layer and request identity. The original layer remains recorded with zero remaining quantity/value. Balance version advances to invalidate old counts. Active reconciled stock, including transferred fragments, returns `hasReconciledCostBasis` and is identified in the UI.

The conservative reconciliation path requires the supplied total to equal the retained historical ledger value. If it differs, `legacy_value_mismatch` leaves all data untouched. A separately designed, reviewed valuation correction is required; this task does not silently rewrite old values or manufacture an offsetting stock movement. A legacy balance requiring several evidenced acquisition layers or a value correction must remain unresolved until that dedicated reconciliation is available. The single documented opening basis must be approved from business evidence by the administrator; the system never pre-fills an average/reference/zero cost. This is an explicit legacy limitation, not a claim of recovered exact history.

## Retry identity

The client generates operation keys internally and derives its attempt identity from all submitted material fields. Unchanged retries reuse the same key after a failure/lost response; changing prices, quantities, warehouse, tracking, destination or notes creates a different key. Pending requests disable editing/Save and prevent modal close. The server hashes the unchanged request before unit conversion; Data never mutates the receipt's input cost. Retail price is included in the canonical hash. Identical completed posts replay without changing pricing, balances, layers, allocations or movements. Legacy unversioned document keys continue to conflict safely.

## Employee workflow

The selected warehouse is the receipt/source context. All operations use real catalog name/barcode search and direct **حفظ**, with Arabic inline errors and retained input on failure. Single variants/units are automatic; multiple active choices are shown. Purchase and selling prices are entered for receipts; acquisition cost is shown only for incoming corrections. Lot/expiry/serial/IMEI controls appear only for persisted tracking requirements, including historical requirements whose global feature was disabled. Required lot identifiers are entered from the goods rather than fabricated. Reason inputs and technical operation-key/review controls are absent. Backend document types/audit reasons remain distinguishable.

Stocktaking shows recorded/count/difference, lot/expiry identity and current purchase/sales/estimated-profit valuation; increased quantities reveal acquisition cost, and changed serial-tracked rows reveal identifiers. Transfers use selected source, destination, product and quantity, with optional notes. History shows local date/time, Arabic movement type, signed quantity, warehouse, related transfer warehouse and notes. It exposes no hashes or operation keys. Light/Dark and RTL use the existing shared styles, modal and scrolling system; no new framework or viewport owner is introduced.
